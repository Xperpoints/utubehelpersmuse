## Quick answer
Use 3 to 5 relevant hashtags per TikTok video: a mix of one or two broad tags and two or three niche tags that describe exactly what the video is about and who it is for. Hashtags help the TikTok algorithm categorize your content for the right test audience, but they are a minor ranking signal — watch time and engagement decide how far a video travels.

## What hashtags actually do on TikTok
Before building a strategy, it helps to understand the job hashtags perform. On TikTok, hashtags are primarily a categorization tool. When you post a video with #homegym and #dumbbellworkout, you are telling the system, in plain text, what this video is about. That helps the [TikTok algorithm](/blog/tiktok-algorithm-explained-2026) place your video in front of people who have watched similar content.

What hashtags do not do is act as a reach multiplier. There is a persistent myth that the right hashtag combination unlocks hidden views, as if TikTok were holding back distribution until you type the magic words. It does not work that way. A video with perfect hashtags and a weak hook dies; a video with no hashtags and a killer hook can still take off, because the algorithm also reads your caption, your on-screen text, the audio, and — most importantly — how viewers behave.

So the goal of a hashtag strategy is accuracy, not volume. You want every tag to answer the question: who is this video for?

## How many hashtags should you use on TikTok?
The honest answer is that TikTok has never given an official number, but creator testing has converged on a practical range: **3 to 5 hashtags per video**. Here is the reasoning.

One hashtag is usually not enough to fully describe a video's topic and audience. Ten or more starts to dilute the signal — each additional tag is another guess about who should see the video, and guesses beyond the first few are usually wrong. Tags also eat into your caption space, and a caption crammed with hashtags looks spammy to viewers, which can cost you the follows and profile visits that actually matter.

A clean, repeatable formula looks like this: one broad category tag, two or three niche tags, and optionally one branded or series tag. For a budget cooking video, that might be #cooking (broad), #budgetmeals #mealpreponabudget #cheapdinners (niche). Every tag describes the same video from a slightly different angle. Nothing is there just because it is trending.

## Niche vs broad hashtags: how to balance them
Broad hashtags — #fyp, #viral, #tiktok, #foryou — have billions of views and essentially no meaning. They describe everyone and therefore no one. Using #fyp does not put you on the For You Page; the algorithm decides that from engagement signals. That said, one broad tag does no real harm and helps with general categorization, so including a single one is fine.

Niche hashtags are where the value lives. Tags like #vanlifebuild, #booktokromance, or #beginnerguitar describe specific communities. Their view counts are smaller, but the people browsing them are exactly the viewers most likely to watch your video to the end — and completion rate is what the algorithm rewards.

The most useful niche tags are the ones your target viewers actually search or follow. Spend twenty minutes browsing your niche and note which tags appear repeatedly on high-performing videos. Those are your core tags. Rotate in one or two experimental tags per video to probe adjacent audiences, and keep notes on which combinations correlate with better reach in your [TikTok analytics](/blog/tiktok-analytics-guide).

## Should you use trending hashtags?
Trending hashtags are tempting because they promise a wave to ride. Sometimes the wave is real: if your content genuinely fits a trend — a trending sound paired with your niche's take on it — the hashtag helps TikTok match you to viewers already engaging with that trend.

But most of the time, trending hashtags are a trap for small creators. A generic trending tag dumps your video into a firehose of unrelated content. Viewers who came for dance trends will not watch your personal finance tips, and their instant swipe-aways tell the algorithm your video is low quality. The hashtag did not boost you; it mislabeled you.

The rule is simple: only use a trending hashtag when a viewer who loves that trend would also love your video. If you have to ask whether it fits, it does not.

## Branded and series hashtags: your long-term asset
A branded hashtag — your username, your show name, your signature series — will not get you discovered, but it builds something more valuable over time: a library. When viewers tap your series hashtag, they land on every episode you have made. "Day 47 of learning to cook" becomes browsable. This compounds. Many creators find that their series hashtag page eventually becomes a binge destination that the algorithm notices, because binge-watching is a retention dream.

Create one branded tag and use it consistently on every relevant video. Put it last in your tag order so the descriptive tags do the discovery work first.

## Where to put hashtags and how to format them
Place hashtags at the end of your caption, after the human-readable part. Your caption's job is to hook the reader and feed [TikTok SEO](/blog/tiktok-seo-keyword-strategy) with natural keywords; a wall of hashtags at the start buries both. Keep tags lowercase or natural case — casing does not affect function. Never put hashtags in comments hoping for extra reach; TikTok reads the video's own caption for categorization.

One formatting note: do not use special characters inside tags, and avoid "banned" tags associated with spam or restricted content. If a hashtag's search page shows no results or a warning, do not use it — pick an alternative.

## Hashtag mistakes that quietly hurt your reach
**Copy-pasting the same 30 tags on every video.** This looks automated and teaches the algorithm nothing new about each video. Vary your tags to match each video's actual topic.

**Using #fyp #viral #foryou as your entire strategy.** These tags are so broad they carry almost no information. One is fine; three is a wasted caption.

**Chasing competitor tags blindly.** Looking at what successful creators in your niche use is smart research — but only adopt tags that accurately describe your content too.

**Ignoring keyword-rich captions.** Hashtags are one categorization input among several. A caption that naturally includes your topic keywords ("easy 15-minute pasta recipe") does more for discovery than five extra hashtags, because TikTok's search increasingly matches plain language. Pair your tag strategy with strong [TikTok captions](/blog/tiktok-captions-guide) and you cover both discovery paths. ## A simple hashtag workflow for every post
Before you post, run through this thirty-second checklist. First, write your caption for humans, including your main keyword naturally. Second, pick one broad tag for your category. Third, pick two or three niche tags your ideal viewer would search. Fourth, add your branded or series tag if you have one. Fifth, read the full set and ask: would someone who loves all of these tags love my video? If yes, post. That five-step habit, repeated across dozens of videos, is what a real TikTok hashtag strategy looks like — not a secret list of magic tags, but consistent, accurate labeling that helps the right strangers find you.
