## Quick answer
YouTube Super Thanks is a tipping feature that lets viewers on a standard video or Short pay to pin a highlighted comment as a thank-you. Super Chat and Super Stickers do the same job during live streams and Premieres. Creators who are eligible can enable all three in YouTube Studio, and the key to earning from them is giving viewers a genuine reason to want to support you.

## What is YouTube Super Thanks, and how is it different from Super Chat and Super Stickers?

Think of YouTube Super Thanks as a digital tip jar attached to your regular videos. When a viewer taps the Thanks button under your video, they choose an amount, type a short message, and that message gets highlighted and pinned in your comments as a colorful, eye-catching post. Everyone who reads the comments sees who supported the video and what they said.

Super Chat and Super Stickers are the older siblings in the same family, but they only work during live streams and Premieres. A Super Chat pins a highlighted message in the live chat feed for a set duration depending on the amount paid, while a Super Sticker sends a fun animated image into the chat. The common thread: viewers pay for attention and to support a creator they enjoy, and creators get an extra revenue stream on top of ads.

The practical difference that matters most is placement. If most of your content is pre-recorded uploads, YouTube Super Thanks is your main fan funding tool. If you go live regularly, Super Chats and Super Stickers will do the heavy lifting. Many creators use both, and each one deserves a slightly different approach.

## Which videos and formats can receive YouTube Super Thanks?

YouTube Super Thanks works on standard long-form videos and on Shorts. That makes it especially useful for creators whose catalog keeps earning views long after upload day. A tutorial you posted eight months ago can still collect Super Thanks today if people are still finding it useful. Super Chat and Super Stickers, by contrast, are only available during live broadcasts, so their earning window closes when the stream ends.

Not every video qualifies. Super Thanks generally cannot appear on videos that are age-restricted, designated as made for kids, private or unlisted, or that fall into other restricted categories. YouTube also reviews your eligibility at the channel level: you need to meet the relevant partner or fan funding requirements in your region, and you need to enable the feature in YouTube Studio under the Monetization tab. If the Thanks button is missing on a video that seems eligible, check that the feature is switched on and that the video itself is public and advertiser-friendly. When in doubt, YouTube's official help pages describe the current eligibility rules.

## How much do creators actually earn from YouTube Super Thanks?

Here is the honest answer: it depends, and you should verify the current terms before you count on any number. YouTube takes a share of every Super Thanks purchase, and payment processing costs can differ by platform (a tip bought on iOS, for example, may go through Apple's in-app purchase system with its own fees). The revenue split can also change over time, so any specific percentage quoted in an older article may be out of date.

That said, most creators find that Super Thanks and the other fan funding features are a meaningful supplement rather than a replacement for ad revenue. A channel with a loyal, engaged audience can see steady trickle income from Super Thanks on popular videos, and a [YouTube money calculator](/tools/youtube-money-calculator) can help you model how different revenue streams add up. Super Chats often spike during big live events, while Super Thanks tends to accumulate slowly across your catalog. Many creators find the real value is diversification: when ad revenue dips for a month, fan funding smooths things out.

One more thing worth knowing: because Super Thanks comments are visible, the feature has a social-proof effect. When viewers see that others have tipped, it normalizes the behavior. Your first few Super Thanks supporters are doing double duty: supporting you financially and signaling to the rest of the audience that tipping is a normal, welcome thing on your channel.

## How to encourage YouTube Super Thanks without begging

Nobody wants to watch a creator guilt-trip their audience. The creators who earn the most from YouTube Super Thanks share a common trait: they make supporting them feel like a genuine, easy choice rather than an obligation. Here is how.

**Thank tippers publicly and specifically.** Read their comment out loud in your next video or pin a reply to their highlighted comment. When viewers see that a Super Thanks gets a real response from you, the feature suddenly has visible value. A generic "thanks to everyone who tipped" once a month is far less effective than naming people and reacting to what they wrote.

**Create moments worth thanking you for.** Super Thanks is a tip, and tips follow good service. The videos that earn the most Super Thanks are usually the ones that solved a real problem: the tutorial that fixed a viewer's broken project, the in-depth comparison that saved them from a bad purchase, the free resource that took you hours to make. When someone comments "this saved me hours," that is your natural opening: a pinned comment from you saying "glad it helped — the Thanks button below helps me keep making these" turns gratitude into action without pressure.

**Explain what the support funds.** Viewers tip more readily when they know where the money goes. "Super Thanks help me hire an editor so I can post twice a week" is concrete and motivating. Vague appeals about "supporting the channel" are not. Tie it to something the audience itself will benefit from.

**Enable it everywhere eligible and mention it lightly.** A surprising number of creators have Super Thanks enabled but never mention it, and an equal number mention it so often it becomes wallpaper. A good rhythm is a brief mention in videos where it makes sense: the end of a particularly helpful tutorial, a community post thanking recent supporters, the description of resource-heavy videos. Mention it like a friendly reminder, not a toll booth.

## Where should you place your Super Thanks call to action?

Placement matters more than people think. The end of the video is the classic spot: the viewer has just received the value, they are in a grateful mood, and a quick line like "if this helped, the Thanks button below is a great way to support the channel" feels natural. Mid-video mentions can work in long tutorials, but keep them brief and tie them to a genuine moment of value.

Your description is underused real estate. A short line near the top of descriptions for high-effort videos ("Enjoyed this? Super Thanks help fund the next deep dive") reaches the many viewers who expand the description for links and timestamps. You can also acknowledge recent Super Thanks supporters in community posts, which gives tippers public appreciation and reminds everyone else the feature exists. If you run [Premieres](/blog/youtube-premiere-guide), mention Super Thanks before the video starts: Premiere audiences arrive early and engaged, and the chat atmosphere makes supporting you feel social rather than solitary.

## Super Thanks vs memberships vs Super Chats: which fits your channel?

These features overlap, so it helps to think about what each one asks of your audience. YouTube Super Thanks asks for a one-time tip on a specific video. It has the lowest commitment, which is why it converts well on tutorial and evergreen content: a viewer tips the video that helped them, not the channel in general.

Super Chats ask for spending in the heat of a live moment. If you run [live streams](/blog/youtube-live-streaming-guide), Super Chats will often outperform Super Thanks simply because the energy is real-time and social. Viewers pay to have their question answered on air or their name shouted out, and the entertainment value is immediate.

Channel memberships ask for a monthly commitment in exchange for perks like badges, emojis, and members-only videos. Memberships are the most powerful fan funding tool for creators with a die-hard community, but they demand ongoing perk delivery. If you cannot reliably produce members-only content, Super Thanks is the safer starting point because it asks for nothing in return except gratitude.

Most creators find a mix works best. Use Super Thanks on your catalog, Super Chats on your streams, and memberships once your community is large and engaged enough to sustain them. ## Common mistakes with YouTube Super Thanks

The biggest mistake is treating Super Thanks as free money and then sounding entitled to it. Phrases like "smash that Thanks button" or implying that real fans tip will actively turn viewers off. Keep the tone grateful, never demanding.

Another common mistake is ignoring the tippers you do get. A Super Thanks comment left unacknowledged is a missed opportunity twice over: the tipper feels invisible, and other viewers see no social reward for tipping. A simple hearted reply costs you ten seconds.

Creators also forget to check where Super Thanks is actually enabled. If you bulk-upload or change a video's settings, verify the feature is on for your best-performing videos, since those are the ones most likely to earn tips. And finally, do not rely on Super Thanks as your primary income plan. It is a wonderful supplement and a strong signal of audience loyalty, but it should sit alongside ads, [sponsorships](/blog/youtube-brand-deals-guide), and other income streams. Diversification is the entire game on YouTube, and fan funding is one of the friendliest pieces of that puzzle.
