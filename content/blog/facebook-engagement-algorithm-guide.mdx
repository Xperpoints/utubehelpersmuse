## Quick answer
The Facebook algorithm ranks Feed posts by predicting which ones will spark meaningful interactions — comments, shares, and real conversations rather than passive likes. It weighs your past relationship with the poster, the content type, recency, and how others are responding. Creators who consistently earn discussion and shares get more reach; creators who chase vanity metrics get less.

## What is the Facebook algorithm, really?

It is not one algorithm. It is a ranking system that scores thousands of candidate posts every time someone opens Facebook and shows them a few hundred, ordered by predicted value. For each post, the system asks a simple question: how likely is this person to have a meaningful interaction with this?

That prediction rests on four buckets of signals. First, **inventory**: everything posted recently by friends, followed pages, and joined groups. Second, **signals**: thousands of data points about the post and the viewer — who posted it, what type of content it is, how old it is, and how the viewer has interacted with similar content before. Third, **predictions**: the system's estimate of how this specific viewer will respond — will they comment, share, watch, hide it, or scroll past? Fourth, **relevance score**: a final ranking that orders the Feed.

The important shift, announced years ago and still central today, is that Facebook optimizes for "meaningful social interactions" rather than raw time spent. A post that triggers a ten-comment discussion between friends outranks a post that gets a hundred silent likes. Everything else in this guide follows from that one idea: the algorithm rewards conversation, not consumption.

## What does "meaningful interactions" actually mean?

In plain terms, meaningful interactions are the actions that show a person genuinely engaged rather than just scrolled past. Ranked roughly by weight:

- **Shares**, especially shares with added commentary. When someone shares your post to their timeline or a group with their own thoughts, they are vouching for it to their whole network.
- **Comments and replies**, with back-and-forth threads counting most. A comment that gets three replies signals a conversation; a lone "nice" signals politeness.
- **Reactions beyond the like**, which carry slightly more information about how content landed.
- **Saves**, a quiet but strong signal that the content was valuable enough to keep.
- **Watch time and completion** on video, which is why the [Facebook video strategy](/blog/facebook-video-strategy-creators) emphasizes retention so heavily.

Notice what is missing from the top of the list: passive views and quick likes. They still count, but they are weak signals. This is why a post with 50 comments routinely outperforms a post with 500 likes. If you remember one sentence from this article, make it this: optimize for discussion, not applause.

## Which ranking signals matter most for creators?

Beyond interaction predictions, several concrete signals shape where your posts land:

**Relationship history.** The algorithm tracks how often a viewer interacts with your page. Someone who commented on your last three posts will see your next one near the top; someone who followed you a year ago and never engaged may rarely see you at all. This is why early engagement on each post matters so much — it re-trains the relationship signal for the next post.

**Content type preference.** If a viewer mostly watches Reels, the Feed shows them more Reels. If they read long text posts in groups, they see more of those. You cannot control viewer habits, but you can diversify formats so different audience segments each get something in their preferred shape. The [Reels strategy guide](/blog/facebook-reels-strategy-2026) covers the discovery side of this.

**Recency.** Fresh posts get a boost, which is why posting when your audience is actually online matters. A great post published at 3 a.m. for your audience decays before most followers wake up. Check the guide on the [best time to post on Facebook](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-facebook) to line up timing with your specific audience.

**Originality.** Facebook has been increasingly open about downranking unoriginal content — reposted viral videos, copied text posts, and compilations scraped from elsewhere. Original posts from the page itself get priority in distribution. If you curate, add genuine commentary that makes the post yours.

**Negative signals.** Hides, "show less" taps, unfollows, and spam reports are powerful downvotes. Clickbait headlines, misleading thumbnails, and engagement bait ("comment YES if you agree") generate exactly the kind of resentment that produces these signals — which is why engagement bait backfires even when it temporarily inflates comments.

## Why is my Facebook reach dropping?

Nine times out of ten, falling reach is falling engagement wearing a disguise. Here is the typical spiral: a page posts content that gets a little less discussion than before, the algorithm shows the next post to slightly fewer people, fewer people means fewer comments, and the cycle continues. It feels like punishment. It is actually just math.

Diagnose before you panic. Open your page insights and compare your last ten posts. Look at comments and shares per post, not just reach — reach is the outcome, engagement is the cause. Common culprits:

- **Content drift.** You started posting what performs for others instead of what your audience followed you for. A cooking page that pivots to generic memes loses the core audience that used to comment.
- **Frequency collapse.** You went from four posts a week to one. The algorithm has less fresh inventory from you, and followers forget to engage, which weakens the relationship signal.
- **Engagement bait hangover.** Old bait tactics ("like if you...") may have worked briefly, then trained your audience to ignore you and trained the algorithm to expect low-quality responses.
- **Format mismatch.** Your audience wants short video but you are still posting text and link cards. Check which formats get your best comments-per-view and shift your mix — [Facebook Stories](/blog/facebook-stories-for-creators) are one format most pages underuse.

One honest exception: overall organic reach for pages has trended down for years as competition for Feed space grows. You are not imagining it. But pages that consistently earn conversation still reach a healthy share of their followers. The bar moved; it did not disappear. The [page growth guide](/blog/facebook-page-growth-guide) has a practical recovery plan if your numbers have been sliding.

## How can creators work with the algorithm instead of against it?

Forget tricks. The algorithm is a mirror of human behavior, so the winning strategy is embarrassingly simple: post things people genuinely want to discuss, and make discussing them easy.

**Ask better questions.** Not "comment YES if you agree" — real questions with no single right answer. "What is the one tool you would never give up?" works because answers differ and people read each other's replies. End how-to posts with a question that invites experience-sharing: "Has this worked for you, or do you do it differently?"

**Post conversation-shaped content.** Opinions with reasoning, before-and-after results, mistakes and lessons, and "unpopular opinion" takes in your niche all invite responses. Tutorials earn saves; debates earn comments; you need both. Aim for a mix across the week rather than one content shape repeated endlessly.

**Reply like a host, not a broadcaster.** The first hour after posting is when the algorithm takes the measure of your post. Reply to early comments with follow-up questions that keep threads alive. A creator who turns five comments into a twenty-reply discussion has done more for distribution than one who collects fifty drive-by likes.

**Use Groups as an engine.** Facebook Groups are where the algorithm's preference for discussion is strongest. A page-linked group gives your most engaged followers a place to talk to each other, and active groups generate the exact signals the Feed rewards. The [Groups growth guide](/blog/facebook-groups-growth-guide) walks through setting one up without it becoming a moderation nightmare.

**Measure what matters.** Track comments, shares, and saves per post with the [engagement rate calculator](/tools/facebook-engagement-rate-calculator) instead of staring at follower counts. A page with 5,000 followers and 8 percent engagement outperforms a page with 50,000 followers and 0.5 percent engagement on every metric that matters, including revenue.

## What should you stop doing?

Short list, high impact. Stop posting the same link five times hoping one version pops — repetition trains followers to tune you out. Stop buying followers or engagement; fake accounts never comment thoughtfully, and they dilute every engagement rate the algorithm watches. Stop copying viral posts word for word; unoriginal content gets downranked and teaches your audience nothing about why they should follow you specifically. And stop treating every post as a broadcast. Facebook is, at its core, still a social network. Pages that behave like community members — asking, answering, reacting, showing up daily — get treated like community members by the ranking system. Pages that behave like billboards get treated like billboards: glanced at, then scrolled past.

The Facebook algorithm is not your enemy and not your friend. It is a prediction machine built on one belief: that conversation is the best measure of value. Build for conversation, measure conversation, and the distribution largely takes care of itself.
