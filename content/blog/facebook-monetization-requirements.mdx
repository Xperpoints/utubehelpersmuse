## Quick answer
Facebook monetization requirements vary by tool: in-stream ads, Stars, and other earning features each have their own eligibility criteria, typically built around your Page or profile's follower count, recent video views or watch time, and compliance with Meta's monetization policies. These thresholds change periodically, so the only reliable move is to check your live eligibility status in the Professional Dashboard or Meta Business Suite. Hitting the numbers gets you in the door — staying policy-compliant keeps the payouts flowing.

## How does Facebook monetization work?
Facebook pays creators through several built-in tools rather than a single partner program. The best-known are in-stream ads, which place short ads inside eligible videos, and Stars, a virtual tipping feature that lets viewers support you during videos and live streams. Meta has also run bonuses and other creator incentives at different times, though availability varies by region and account.

The important thing to understand is that each tool is gated separately. Qualifying for one does not automatically unlock the others — each carries its own set of facebook monetization requirements, and each is evaluated against your recent performance. A Page that qualifies for Stars may still need to grow before in-stream ads open up, and vice versa.

Another key point: monetization on Facebook is tied to Pages and professional-mode profiles, not casual personal use. If you're serious about earning, make sure you're operating from a Page (or professional mode) that publishes original content consistently. A tool like the [Facebook engagement rate calculator](/tools/facebook-engagement-rate-calculator) can help you gauge whether your audience is engaged enough to sustain a monetized Page, since engaged audiences drive both eligibility metrics and actual revenue.

## What are the Facebook monetization requirements for in-stream ads?
In-stream ads are usually the first revenue stream creators aim for, and the eligibility bar reflects that. Meta has historically tied in-stream ads eligibility to a minimum follower count, a minimum amount of watch time on your videos over a recent window, and a minimum number of qualifying videos published. Meta also requires that you operate in an eligible country and comply with its monetization policies.

Here is the catch: the specific numbers have changed more than once. Articles and videos quoting exact follower counts or minute-viewed thresholds may already be out of date by the time you read them. Rather than memorizing a number, build toward the general shape of the requirement — a real audience of engaged followers, a library of original videos that people actually watch, and a clean compliance record. Then verify the current thresholds on Meta's official monetization eligibility pages or inside your own Professional Dashboard, which shows your progress toward each criterion.

If you're building from zero, treat this as a content strategy question. Original long-form videos — explainers, commentary, documentaries, scripted series — accumulate the watch time that short clips and text posts don't. A consistent posting rhythm, planned around the [best time to post on Facebook](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-facebook), helps each video reach its full potential. And if you're comparing platforms, our breakdown of [YouTube monetization requirements](/blog/youtube-monetization-requirements-explained) shows how Facebook's bar stacks up against YouTube's.

## Do Facebook Stars have separate monetization requirements?
Yes. Stars are Facebook's built-in tipping system: viewers buy Star packs and send them during your videos, Reels, and live streams, and Meta pays you per Star received under its payout terms. Unlike ads, Stars don't depend on advertisers — they depend on how much your audience likes you, which makes them a loyalty metric as much as a revenue stream.

Eligibility for Stars generally follows the same pattern as other tools: a follower minimum, compliance with monetization policies, and operation in a supported country. The follower bar for Stars has historically sat lower than the bar for in-stream ads, which is why many creators unlock Stars first. Once again, confirm the live criteria in your dashboard instead of trusting third-party figures, because Meta revises these numbers.

Maximizing Stars is a community-building exercise. Creators who do well with Stars tend to stream regularly, acknowledge supporters by name on air, and build small rituals around tipping — shout-out segments, milestone celebrations, supporter-only perks. Many creators find that a small, devoted audience sends more Stars than a large, passive one, which is another reason engagement rate matters more than raw follower count when you're working toward monetization.

## Where do you check your monetization eligibility?
Meta gives you two official places to see exactly where you stand, and you should bookmark both:

1. **Professional Dashboard** — on mobile, this is the hub for creators. It shows which monetization tools you're eligible for, which ones you're close to unlocking, and your progress toward each criterion.
2. **Meta Business Suite / Monetization Manager** — on desktop, the monetization section shows eligibility status per tool, any policy violations, and your payout settings.

Check these directly rather than relying on screenshots from tutorials. If a tool shows as unavailable, the dashboard tells you which criterion you haven't met yet — that becomes your to-do list. When everything shows green but the tool still isn't active, the usual culprits are an incomplete payout setup or a pending review, both of which the dashboard will flag.

Make this a monthly habit even after you're monetized. Eligibility is re-evaluated on rolling windows, so a slow month can put a tool back into "not eligible" status. Catching that early lets you adjust your content plan before revenue actually dips.

## What policies can disqualify your Page?
Meeting the numerical thresholds means nothing if your content violates monetization policies. Meta's rules go further than the standard community guidelines — content can be perfectly allowed on Facebook yet still ineligible for monetization. The most common disqualifiers for creators:

- **Unoriginal or repurposed content.** Re-uploading viral clips, compilations of other people's videos, or lightly edited reposts is the fastest way to lose monetization access. Facebook's systems are aggressive about detecting this.
- **Limited originality.** Slideshows of static images, text-to-speech narration over stock footage with no added value, and mass-produced low-effort content can all be flagged.
- **Policy-violating content.** Misinformation, sensational health claims, and graphic material risk both strikes and demonetization.
- **Artificial behavior.** Buying followers, joining engagement pods, or running fake giveaways can trigger violations that lock you out of monetization entirely.

The honest path is also the durable one: publish original videos you made, keep your Page's record clean, and treat every upload as something a reviewer would be comfortable showing an advertiser. If monetization is your goal, read Meta's monetization policies in full before you invest months of work — it's far cheaper to adjust early than to appeal a demonetization later.

Focus on formats that hold attention, because watch time is the engine of eligibility; study your retention graphs and double down on whatever keeps viewers watching. Diversify formats while you grow. Finally, keep your expectations honest. Monetization rewards Pages that already create value for an audience; it rarely rescues a Page that doesn't. Build the audience first, keep every video original and policy-safe, verify the current thresholds on Meta's official pages, and eligibility becomes a milestone on a road you were already walking — not a lottery ticket.
